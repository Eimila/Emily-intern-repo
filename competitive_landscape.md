# Competitive Landscape: Focus Bear vs. Other Productivity Apps

## What each app actually does

**Freedom** — A pure website/app blocker. You schedule or start sessions that block chosen sites and apps across Mac, Windows, iOS, Android, and Chrome, with cross-device session syncing, an "allow-only" mode, and focus sounds. It doesn't touch routines or habit-building — it's a blocking tool, full stop.

**Cold Turkey** — Also a blocker, but built for people who don't trust themselves to stop. Its signature feature is a lock mode that's nearly impossible to disable once started, plus "Frozen Turkey," which can lock you out of your entire computer for a scheduled break. One-time purchase, all data stored locally, Pomodoro timer included.

**Tiimo** — A visual, ADHD-oriented planner. Its core strength is an AI Co-Planner that takes a spoken or typed brain-dump and breaks it into a color-coded, emoji-tagged visual timeline, plus a focus timer and home-screen widgets. It's about making time and tasks visible and reducing the friction of planning, not about blocking distractions.

**Routinery** — A routine/ritual builder. You assemble a sequence of timed steps (e.g., a morning routine), and the app voice-guides you through each one, tracks streaks, and gamifies consistency with badges and a motivational chatbot. Its focus is habit consistency through repetition and gamification.

**Focus Bear** — Combines habit/routine building, cross-device distraction blocking (Super Pomodoro Mode), and behavioral nudges (like call-aware break reminders and the "Late No More" alert system) in one app, purpose-built for ADHD/AuDHD brains rather than a general productivity audience.

**Forest (Focus Forest)** — A gamified focus timer. You plant a virtual tree, set a session length (10–90 minutes, default 25), and if you leave the app to get distracted, the tree dies. Successful sessions earn coins to unlock new tree species and build out a virtual forest, with an Allow List for apps you're permitted to use mid-session. Forest also partners with a real-world tree-planting organization, so accumulated focus time can fund actual trees being planted. It's purely a motivation/gamification layer on top of a timer — no routines, no cross-device blocking, no ADHD-specific behavioral features.

## Reflection

**What makes Focus Bear different from these apps?**
Most competitors specialize in one lane: Freedom and Cold Turkey are blockers only, Tiimo is a visual planner only, Routinery is a routine/gamification tool only. Focus Bear tries to cover routine-building, distraction-blocking, and break/accountability nudges together in a single app, and it's designed specifically around ADHD/AuDHD needs (e.g., call-aware breaks, escalating "Late No More" alerts) rather than being a generic focus tool that happens to work for everyone.

**If you were a user, why would you choose Focus Bear over competitors?**
If I only needed blocking, I'd reach for Freedom or Cold Turkey. If I only needed a nicer visual planner, Tiimo. But if I wanted one app that builds my morning/evening routines, blocks distractions during focus time, and actively nudges me back on track without me stitching three separate apps together, Focus Bear is the more complete option — and it's built with ADHD-specific pain points in mind rather than bolted on as an afterthought.

**What's one feature that other apps have that Focus Bear doesn't?**
Cold Turkey's lock mode (and its "Frozen Turkey" full-computer lockout) is more extreme than anything Focus Bear currently offers. For someone who will actively try to bypass their own blocks — a very real ADHD pattern — Cold Turkey's near-unbreakable enforcement is a genuine edge Focus Bear doesn't match. Forest's gamified "grow a tree, kill it if you leave" mechanic, plus its tie-in to funding real tree planting, is also a simple, emotionally engaging motivator that Focus Bear doesn't have an equivalent of.

**Based on your research, what's one improvement you think Focus Bear could make?**
Tiimo's AI Co-Planner — turning a messy brain-dump into a broken-down, time-estimated plan through conversation — targets task initiation, which is one of the hardest ADHD struggles and arguably upstream of needing to block distractions at all. Focus Bear could benefit from a similar AI-assisted task breakdown feature, so it helps users start the work, not just stay on it once they've started.
