# Agile Workflows & Kanban

## Research & Learn

How does a Kanban board work, and how does it help manage workflow?

A Kanban board is a visual tool that represents work as cards moving through a series of columns, each representing a stage in the workflow. It helps manage workflow by making all work visible in one place, so the team can see at a glance what's being worked on, what's waiting, and what's finished, without needing status meetings or separate tracking documents. This visibility makes bottlenecks obvious as soon as cards start piling up in one column.

What do the different columns on a Kanban board represent?

Columns typically map to the stages a task passes through from idea to completion. Common examples include Backlog, for work that has been identified but not yet started; In Progress, for work actively being done; Blocked, for work that has stalled due to a dependency or issue; In Review, for work awaiting feedback or approval; and Done, for completed work. Teams customize these columns to match their actual process.

How do tasks move through the board, and who is responsible for updating them?

Tasks move left to right as their status changes, typically pulled forward by the person doing the next stage of work rather than pushed by a manager. The person actively working on a task is generally responsible for updating its status, since they have the most accurate, real-time knowledge of where it stands. This keeps the board honest and up to date without relying on a separate person to chase updates.

What are the benefits of limiting work in progress (WIP)?

Limiting WIP caps how many tasks can sit in an active column at once. This forces the team to finish existing work before starting new work, which reduces context-switching, surfaces bottlenecks faster since a full column signals a problem immediately, and improves overall throughput because tasks move through the system faster than if everyone worked on many things at once.

## Reflection

How does Kanban help manage priorities and avoid overload?

Kanban makes capacity visible. Because WIP limits cap how much can be in progress at once, the team is forced to finish or actively address existing work before pulling in new tasks, which prevents anyone from being quietly overloaded with too many parallel tasks. It also makes it easy to see what's next in the backlog, so prioritization happens continuously rather than only at planning meetings.

How can you improve your workflow using Kanban principles?

I can improve my workflow by setting a personal WIP limit on how many tasks I allow myself to have in progress at once, and by updating card status the moment my work on a task changes state rather than batching updates later. This keeps the board accurate for teammates who depend on it and reduces the temptation to start new work before finishing what's already active.
