# Roles in an Agile Team

## Research and Learn

What are the key roles in an Agile team, and what does each role do?

Developers write, test, and ship the actual product code. On a mobile team this includes iOS and Android developers who build features, fix bugs, and maintain the codebase, working closely with design and QA to make sure what ships matches what was intended.

QA (Quality Assurance) verifies that features work as expected before and after release. QA writes and runs test cases, catches regressions, reports bugs with enough detail for developers to reproduce them, and helps define what "done" actually means for a piece of work.

Product Manager (PM) owns the product roadmap and decides what gets built and why. The PM gathers requirements from users and stakeholders, prioritizes the backlog, writes specs or user stories, and makes trade-off calls between features when time or resources are limited.

Agile Project Manager (Agile PM) focuses on how work gets done rather than what gets built. This role facilitates ceremonies such as stand-ups, planning, and retrospectives, removes blockers for the team, tracks progress on the board, and helps keep the process running smoothly. In Scrum this is closer to the Scrum Master role.

Other supporting roles include stakeholders, who have an interest in the outcome and provide input or approval but aren't doing the day-to-day work; UX/UI designers, who design the interface and user flows that developers implement; and customer support, who are often the first to hear about bugs or pain points from real users and feed that information back into the backlog.

How do these roles collaborate in Kanban vs. Scrum?

In Scrum, collaboration is structured around the sprint. The PM and team agree on a sprint's worth of work during sprint planning, developers and QA work through it together during the sprint, and everyone syncs daily at stand-up. Roles are more formally defined, especially the Scrum Master, whose job is specifically to protect the process and the team's focus.

In Kanban, collaboration is continuous rather than batched. There's no fixed sprint boundary, so the PM prioritizes the backlog on an ongoing basis, developers pull the next-highest-priority item when they have capacity, and QA reviews items as they move into a review column rather than waiting for a sprint to end. Roles tend to be less formally defined, and communication happens more through the board and async updates than through a fixed set of ceremonies.

## Reflection

What is your role, and how does it contribute to the Agile process?

I'm an iOS developer. My role contributes to the Agile process by turning prioritized backlog items into working, shippable features on iOS. Because the team works in small increments, I focus on delivering one testable piece of functionality at a time rather than building a large feature in isolation, which keeps my work reviewable and lets issues get caught early instead of after months of work.

How do your responsibilities overlap or interact with other roles in the team?

My work overlaps most with QA, since every feature or fix I build needs to be verified before it's considered done, and with design, since I need accurate specs and assets to implement the UI correctly. I also interact with the PM when requirements are unclear or when a technical constraint on iOS affects what's feasible, and occasionally with customer support when a bug report needs more detail before I can reproduce and fix it.

## Task

Write a short description of how your role fits into the Agile team.

As an iOS developer, I take prioritized items from the backlog and turn them into working features on the iOS app. I move my own cards through the board as I pick up work, build it, and hand it off for review, and I flag blockers early rather than sitting on them, since the team's flow depends on issues surfacing quickly.

Identify one other role you frequently collaborate with and describe how you can improve teamwork with them.

I collaborate most often with QA. To improve teamwork with QA, I can write clearer PR descriptions and testing notes when I move a card to Ready for Review, including what changed, what edge cases I already tested, and how to reproduce the original issue if it's a bug fix. This should reduce back-and-forth and help QA verify my work faster.
