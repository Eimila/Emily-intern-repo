# AI Usage Guidelines

## Research & Learn

**AI tools typically used in this role**
For an intern-level role like mine, the most common uses are code/syntax lookup and debugging help, drafting and editing written content (emails, documentation, reflections), summarizing or organizing research material, and brainstorming or structuring ideas before implementation.

**Benefits and risks of using AI in a professional setting**
AI can significantly speed up repetitive or well-defined tasks — looking up syntax, summarizing large amounts of text, drafting first passes of documents — freeing up time for higher-value thinking. The risks are: accepting AI output without verification (AI can be confidently wrong or incomplete), leaking confidential or sensitive company/user data by pasting it into external tools, and skipping the learning process by letting AI solve problems you should understand yourself, which erodes skill development over time.

**Information that should never be entered into AI tools**
Confidential company data (financials, unreleased product plans, internal credentials or API keys), personally identifiable user data, security-sensitive configuration or infrastructure details, and anything covered by an NDA or access restriction. If in doubt about whether something is sensitive, the safe default is not to paste it into an AI tool.

**Fact-checking and validating AI-generated content**
Cross-check factual claims against primary or authoritative sources rather than trusting the AI's summary alone. For code, actually run and test it rather than assuming it works. For research summaries, check whether the AI's coverage is complete — AI tools often have blind spots (e.g., over-indexing on the most commonly discussed / English-language sources) that need to be filled in manually.

## Reflection

**When should you use AI for assistance, and when should you rely on your own skills?**
I use AI for questions that have a clear, factual answer — for example, code syntax questions. But when it comes to designing or implementing a feature, that needs to be worked out based on the actual requirements, not handed straight to AI as a question to solve for me.

**How can you avoid over-reliance on AI while still benefiting from it?**
Continuous learning is what keeps me from over-relying on AI. I make a point of systematically learning the skills my work actually requires — for example, learning how to use Git properly to manage code — rather than asking AI to generate a terminal command and running it without understanding why it works. The goal is to use AI to increase my efficiency, not to just focus on whatever output it produces.

**What steps will you take to ensure data privacy when using AI tools?**
I treat AI tools as operating inside a strict data sandbox: I don't grant them access to view or modify sensitive data, and any action that touches sensitive systems or information gets checked with my mentor before I proceed.

## Task

**Task I improved using an AI tool**
I used AI to help organize research notes on Focus Bear's competitors. Within about a minute, it returned detailed profiles for three companies, each structured consistently into company overview, product description, and strengths/weaknesses analysis.

**Critical review of the AI-generated output**
The output looked polished, but on review I noticed a real gap: the AI had only covered popular Western/European-American market competitors, missing the Asia-market side of the landscape entirely. I fact-checked this by adding a competitor I know personally — a focus/productivity app that's especially popular among students in the Asian market — along with my own genuine first-hand experience using it and a comparison against Focus Bear's product. This correction was the single biggest edit I made to the AI's output, and it's also my concrete example of following Focus Bear's AI usage guidelines: using AI to speed up the first draft, then critically checking its coverage and filling in what it missed rather than accepting it at face value.

**Best practice I will follow when using AI tools at Focus Bear**
Always treat AI-generated research as a first draft, not a final answer — actively check it for coverage gaps (e.g., regional or market blind spots) and correct them with real, verified knowledge before treating the output as complete.
