# What Do You Want to Learn & Contribute?

## What do you hope to learn from this internship?

I hope to experience what real-world software development actually looks like — not just writing code on my own, but building features within a live product used by real people, and working through the full process alongside a team. Coming from an economics background and being in my first semester of a CS master's, I'm especially keen to understand how professional engineering teams turn ideas and requirements into working, shipped features.

## What skills are you most excited to develop?

I'm most excited to develop my Swift and macOS development skills, since that's the core of my role here. I'm also looking forward to getting comfortable with real Git collaboration workflows — branching, pull requests, code review — which is quite different from writing code by yourself. Learning to work this way within a team is something I really want to get right.

## What do you think you can contribute to the team?

Beyond my development work, I think I can bring a genuine understanding of the people Focus Bear is built for. I personally relate to many of the challenges the app is designed to solve, so I have real empathy for the users and a strong motivation to make the product genuinely helpful for them. I hope that perspective can be useful when thinking through how features actually feel to use.

## What is one area you feel less confident in that you'd like to improve?

Because I'm switching into CS and I'm only in my first semester, I'm still building confidence in some engineering practices — for example, navigating a large existing codebase and going deeper into a specific framework. These are areas I'm aware I need to strengthen, and I see this internship as a great opportunity to improve them by working on real code with guidance from the team.

## What does success in this internship look like for you?

Success would mean being able to independently complete the development tasks I'm assigned, contributing a feature that actually ships in the product, and becoming a smooth, reliable collaborator within the team — rather than just finishing the onboarding checklist. If I can grow into someone the team can hand real work to and trust to deliver, I'll consider this internship a success.
