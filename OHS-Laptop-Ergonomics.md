# OHS: Laptop Ergonomics

## Research & Learn

**Risks of using a laptop without an external monitor or keyboard**
When a laptop's screen and keyboard are fixed together, you can't position both correctly at the same time. Looking down at a low screen strains the neck and upper back, while typing on an attached keyboard often forces the shoulders up and the wrists to bend. Over time this combination leads to neck and shoulder tension, lower back pain from slouching toward the low screen, and wrist/forearm strain from awkward typing angles. Prolonged static posture also reduces blood flow and increases fatigue and eye strain.

**Ergonomic equipment that improves posture**
A laptop stand (or a stack of books) raises the screen to eye level, paired with an external keyboard and mouse so the hands can stay at elbow height. An external monitor gives more flexibility for screen height and distance. An ergonomic or adjustable chair supports the lower back and lets feet rest flat on the floor. A footrest can help if the chair or desk height doesn't allow that. A document holder can reduce neck twisting when referencing paper notes.

**Adjustments to monitor height, chair position, and desk setup**
The top of the screen should sit roughly at or just below eye level, about an arm's length away, so the neck stays neutral. The chair should be adjusted so hips and knees are at roughly 90 degrees, feet flat on the floor, and the lower back supported. The desk height should let forearms rest parallel to the floor while typing, with elbows close to the body rather than reaching forward or up.

**Daily habits that reduce the impact of prolonged laptop use**
Taking a short movement break (standing, stretching, walking) every 30–60 minutes counters the effects of static sitting. Periodic posture checks help catch slouching before it becomes a habit. The 20-20-20 rule (every 20 minutes, look at something 20 feet away for 20 seconds) reduces eye strain. Alternating between sitting and standing, and consciously relaxing the shoulders and wrists, also helps prevent cumulative strain.

## Reflection

**Equipment changes**
My workspace is a library study desk with no external monitor or keyboard. Since I can't bring in separate peripherals, the main lever I have is adjusting the desk height itself to bring the laptop screen and typing position closer to a neutral posture.

**Behavioural changes**
I've set up a sit-reminder on my smartwatch that prompts me to stand and move for two minutes whenever I've been sitting too long. This directly targets the risk of prolonged static posture that comes with laptop-only setups.

**Reminders to maintain posture and take breaks**
I use Focus Bear alongside the smartwatch reminders. Beyond the movement prompts, Focus Bear's timer keeps me consciously aware that I'm in a work session, which has sharpened my sense of purpose while working and made me far less prone to distraction.
