# Professionalism at Focus Bear — Reflection

## Have you ever experienced or witnessed great teamwork and professionalism? What made it effective?

Yes. I previously worked in an audit team, and the best part of that experience was how clear the team's structure was — there was a well-defined work plan and clear task allocation, so everyone knew exactly what they were responsible for. On top of that, every day ended with each person reporting their work to the supervisor, and before doing anything uncertain, people would always check first. That habit of confirming before acting meant everyone stayed aligned and could be confident they were doing the right thing. Looking back, what made it effective was the combination of clarity, consistent daily communication, and a culture where asking first was normal rather than a sign of weakness.

## What steps can you take to make sure your communication is clear, respectful, and inclusive?

- Come to meetings with a short agenda and clear questions, rather than expecting others to lead everything.
- Do a quick search on basic questions before asking, so I'm using people's time on the things that genuinely need discussion.
- Take notes during meetings and follow up on my action items, so we don't loop back over the same ground each week.
- Stay focused in meetings and avoid multi-tasking, so people feel heard.
- When I'm unsure of a direction, frame it as "Based on my research, I think I should do X next — is that what you'd recommend?" rather than either going silent or going rogue.
- Choose the right communication channel for the urgency, so I'm respectful of people's time and attention.

## How can you help create a positive, fun, and professional work environment?

I can contribute by staying curious and asking thoughtful questions, sharing my ideas openly, and giving regular updates so teammates and supervisors always know where things stand. I want to be someone who receives feedback constructively and adjusts, rather than getting defensive. Focus Bear values a relaxed, supportive culture where you can be yourself while still being accountable, so I'd like to match that — being friendly and easy to work with, while still being reliable and respectful of everyone on the team.

## How can you prepare effectively for meetings with supervisors and colleagues?

- Read the project brief and onboarding material beforehand, and make sure I've actually used the app and looked at the website.
- Come with questions and my own agenda, and know my current progress and any blockers before the meeting starts.
- Do a bit of homework before asking, so I'm not taking up time on things I could have found myself.
- Take careful notes during the meeting and follow up on action items afterwards.
- When sending calendar invites, include the specific project name and a short agenda — not a vague title like "Focus Bear" or "Client meeting" — since the team runs multiple intern projects.

## What would it look like to be proactive during your internship?

Being proactive means not waiting around to be told what to do. It looks like taking initiative, becoming increasingly self-led as I grow more confident, and giving regular updates — ideally at least once at the end of each working day — without needing to be chased. When I hit a problem, being proactive also means giving it a real go myself first: searching online or asking ChatGPT, then asking a teammate, and only reaching out to my supervisor if I'm still blocked after around 30 minutes of honest effort. It also means speaking up with suggestions when I'm unsure of the direction, rather than staying idle.

## How might you follow up with colleagues or supervisors if you need information or action from them? How would you escalate, and when?

I'd escalate based on urgency, starting with the lightest touch and moving up only if needed:

- **Email** for general communication, expecting a reply within about 48 hours.
- **Discord/Teams** for medium-priority things that need a response within 24 hours.
- **SMS** for urgent matters that need a reply within about 2 hours.
- **A phone call** for a genuine emergency.

Most things aren't urgent, so I'd default to the lightest option first and simply follow up with a friendly nudge if something was missed. But if a matter is genuinely time-sensitive and I'm not getting a response, I'd move up the chain rather than waiting on an email reply while something is on fire.

## What are some things you won't do?

- I won't sit idle waiting for instructions — I'll take initiative or ask a teammate if I'm unsure.
- I won't come to meetings unprepared or try to "wing it."
- I won't skip taking notes and then repeat the same questions later.
- I won't ask questions I could answer myself with a 10-second search.
- I won't ghost my project — even a quick "still working on it" keeps everyone in sync.
- I won't ignore feedback or action items.
- I won't rely on only one form of communication when something is urgent.
- I won't send vague calendar invites without a clear subject and agenda.
- And more broadly, I won't engage in discrimination, bullying, or toxic behaviour — I want to be respectful and inclusive towards everyone on the team.
